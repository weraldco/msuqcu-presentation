Your Path Doesn't Have to Be Straight

What Rejection, Passion, and Community Taught Me

1. So... What Was College Like in 2007?

Visual: Old campus / graduation photo

"First of all, yes... this is actually me." 😂

"And yes, this picture is from 2011. Please don't ask why the quality
is so bad. We didn't have AI to fix everything back then."

"When I was in college, I thought I had a pretty good idea of what my

You don't need to have your entire future figured out while you're in
college.

5. 🤝 Then I Found My People

Visual: Community/event photos

"One of the best things I did was start joining tech communities."

"And I realized something: there are a lot of people just like me."

"Some were beginners. Some were experienced developers. Some were just
there for the food." 😂

"And that's okay. You can learn something from all of them."

What community gave me

People --- people who share the same interests

Learning --- people who can teach you things you don't know

Opportunities --- events, jobs, projects, and collaborations

Confidence --- a place to contribute even while you're still
learning

Connections --- people who can become friends, mentors,
teammates, or future colleagues

"You never know who is sitting beside you."

Visuals:

Community event photo - First tech job offer - Promotion/career
milestone - Volunteer/community work

Don't present these as a list of achievements. Present them as examples
of what happened after you started putting yourself out there.

6. 🌱 So What Did I Learn?

1. Don't worry about having everything figured out.

"Seriously. I didn't."

You're a student. You're supposed to be learning and exploring.

2. Try things.

"Join an organization. Attend an event. Build something. Break
something. Hopefully don't break production." 😂

You don't need to know whether something will become your career.
Sometimes you discover your passion simply by trying.

3. Don't be afraid of rejection.

"You're going to hear no. Sometimes multiple times. Keep applying."

A rejection is an event---not your identity.

4. Don't compare your timeline.

"Someone might get their dream job at 21. Someone might find theirs at 30. Someone might change careers at 35."

"Apparently, I'm proof of that." 😂

There is no universal timeline for success.

5. Find your community.

"Don't just collect certificates. Collect people."

Learn from them. Help them. Grow with them.

Presentation Rhythm

Keep the talk conversational rather than overly motivational.

Funny → Story → Lesson → Funny → Story → Lesson → Inspiration

The goal isn't for students to think:

"That was an impressive career story."

The goal is for them to leave thinking:

"Maybe I don't need to have everything figured out either."
